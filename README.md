Learn how therapists can avoid AI-related HIPAA violations, evaluate ChatGPT and alternative tools, and protect patient information with practical security measures.

Sharing this information with an AI platform without appropriate safeguards can create HIPAA compliance problems.</span></p><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">The challenge is not simply deciding whether AI is useful. Problems arise when therapists use technology without appropriate privacy and security safeguards.</span></p><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">Under HIPAA, covered healthcare providers must protect PHI from unauthorized access, disclosure, and use. When an AI platform receives identifiable patient information, its handling of that information becomes an important compliance consideration.</span></p><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">Here are five situations that can create HIPAA risks.</span></p><h3 dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;"><strong>1. Entering Patient Information Into Public AI Tools</strong></span></h3><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">A therapist might paste a patient's session notes into an AI chatbot and ask it to generate a progress summary. Using AI Transcription Without Proper Safeguards</strong></span></h3><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">AI transcription tools can convert therapy conversations into written notes. However, recordings may contain highly sensitive information, including personal experiences, diagnoses, and treatment details.</span></p><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">Therapists should evaluate recording consent requirements, vendor agreements, data storage, and access controls before using these tools.</span></p><h3 dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;"><strong>3. Working With Vendors Without a Business Associate Agreement</strong></span></h3><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">If an AI vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity or business associate, a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) may be required.</span></p><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">A vendor's general privacy policy is not a substitute for a BAA when HIPAA requires one.</span></p><h3 dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;"><strong>4. Ignoring Data Retention and Security Policies</strong></span></h3><p dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;">Some platforms may retain prompts, uploaded files, or generated content. Therapists should understand how long information is stored, who can access it, and whether it can be deleted.</span></p><h3 dir="ltr"><span style="font-family: 'book antiqua', palatino, serif;"><strong>5.
